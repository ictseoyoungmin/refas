# Isolated visual acceptance review

## Scope and method

I reviewed only `REQUIREMENTS.md`, the two manifests, both complete `source.png` sheets, all five comparison boards, and every individual image in the packet's `A/` and `B/` directories. I did not consult authoring history, candidate mappings, other reports, repository files or technical validation results. Candidate labels in this report are only the anonymous labels supplied in the packet.

This is a visual assessment of source-specific identity and major construction **at the declared prototype scope**. It is not a mesh, hierarchy, connectivity, dimensional, articulation-range or production-readiness certification. Source cameras are not registered to render cameras, so I judge proportions and silhouette qualitatively. Render noise, dark interiors and occlusion limit some conclusions.

In the tables, **met** means the requirement is sufficiently demonstrated for this visual prototype scope; **partial** means its main idea is present but visibly incomplete or simplified; **missing** means the necessary visible construction is absent in an inspection view; and **uncertain** means the images cannot decide it. A partial finish detail does not automatically defeat prototype acceptance. A major missing source construction does.

## Decisions

| Asset | Relative preference | Candidate A: declared visual prototype criterion | Candidate B: declared visual prototype criterion | Finished-art acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jacket | **A** | **Meets**, with visible refinement needs | **Does not yet meet**: the defining ornament assembly is incompletely constructed | Neither candidate is accepted as finished art |
| Cybernetic arm | **B** | **Does not yet meet**: rear actuator exposure and the service compartment are materially incomplete | **Meets**, with visible refinement needs | Neither candidate is accepted as finished art |

These acceptance decisions are independent of the relative preferences. In particular, a complete but simply surfaced prototype can meet the criterion; a more decorated object with a missing major compartment cannot.

## 1. Jacket

### Relative preference: A

Candidate A more convincingly preserves this source's distinctive uniform-jacket identity. In `garment/A/ornament.png`, the larger sleeve shield, smaller chest shield, recognizable fleur-de-lis motifs, two draped chains and hanging pendants form one readable assembly. The chains visibly reach the sleeve shield. `garment/A/back.png` and `garment/A/left.png` also retain the rear shoulder chain detail on the same decorated side.

Candidate B retains the general position of the two shields, but `garment/B/ornament.png` shows the two chain runs ending below and to the inner side of the sleeve shield, without a visible receiving attachment. The uppermost links near the chest shield also appear as separated ovals. Its simplified line emblem and missing hanging pendants remove much of the specific ornament's identity. `garment/B/back.png` lacks the visible rear shoulder chain assembly present in the source's back view. This is more than a difference in metal shading or decorative density: the primary ornament is not coherently assembled in the supplied view.

A also has a more readable turned lapel in `garment/A/fold-side.png` and `garment/A/fold-oblique.png`. B's corresponding lapel reads as a thin angular blade, and the collar's lower rim stands clear of the shoulder/neck transition in the side and oblique inspections. B's full upper piping is more continuous in front, which is a local advantage, but it does not outweigh A's stronger fold and ornament construction.

The basic body and sleeve silhouette is similar in both candidates. Both show a cropped, tapered torso, long sleeves, an open front, open cuff rims and a fitted back. Neither is merely a collection of isolated cylindrical placeholders. The natural cloth shaping is much simpler than the source, but that alone does not prevent a prototype pass.

### Major blockers and prototype judgment

**A meets the declared visual prototype criterion.** The essential jacket silhouette, hollow opening, collar/lapels, sleeves, cuff openings, straps/buckles and source-defining decoration are present and visually coherent. I do not identify a demonstrated major source or construction omission that prevents the narrower prototype acceptance.

A still needs refinement. The lapel outline and piping need a more consistent relationship, the sleeve shape is unusually smooth and straight, and some transitions below the lapels remain blunt. The rear chain assembly is a simplified interpretation rather than an exact reproduction. Those are real source differences, but the major garment and ornament construction remains legible.

**B does not yet meet the declared criterion.** Its main blocker is the incompletely constructed source-defining shield/chain assembly in `garment/B/ornament.png`: the chain runs terminate short of the sleeve crest, the characteristic hanging pendants are missing, and the back view does not retain the rear decoration. Correcting the emblem drawing alone would not fix the visibly incomplete chain assembly. `garment/B/fold-side.png` and `garment/B/fold-oblique.png` add a separate concern about the collar/lapel transition. I cannot certify that those parts are disconnected, but their visible relationship is less convincingly cloth construction than A's.

The decorated-side choice is accepted in both candidates. I have not treated the source's inconsistent oblique ornament placement as a requirement to duplicate decoration onto both sides.

### Separate finished-art judgment

**Neither is accepted as finished art.** A is the stronger prototype, but its broad smooth fabric surfaces, restrained sleeve deformation, blunt panel transitions and partially unresolved piping do not yet achieve the source's tailored cloth finish. B additionally needs the ornament assembly repaired, the collar transition resolved and the broad straps' material reading corrected. This finished-art judgment does not overturn A's prototype acceptance, and it is not the reason B fails the narrower criterion.

## 2. Cybernetic arm

### Relative preference: B

Candidate B better interprets the source's nested armor, exposed actuator frame and service access. The clearest comparisons are `automail/A/mechanism-detail.png` against `automail/B/mechanism-detail.png`, and the two `service-open.png` images.

In A, a large uninterrupted graphite polygonal member covers the rear upper arm. The side rods are present, and some mechanism is visible from the front, but the source's exposed rear actuator core is replaced visually by a solid cover. B shows a central cylinder, exposed rod, surrounding side members and upper bracket, with readable spaces between them. That retains the source's mechanical organization even though the parts are simplified.

The opened service cover is even more decisive. A's removed cover exposes another continuous solid faceted surface. B exposes a framed recessed compartment containing two cylinders and supporting elements, and the removed cover has visible inner ribs. This is the source's service-access construction in a credible prototype interpretation.

`automail/B/socket-depth.png` and `automail/B/armor-depth.png` also show more readable shell layering. B's hand has slimmer individual digits and a more segmented dorsal plate arrangement in `hand-front.png`. A's dorsal hand is smoother in places, but its finger blocks and joints are thicker and its palm pads more bulbous. Both remain visibly simplified hands.

### Major blockers and prototype judgment

**A does not yet meet the declared visual prototype criterion.** Two named construction gaps prevent acceptance:

1. **The rear exposed actuator structure is substantially covered by a solid mass.** This is visible in `automail/A/back.png` and especially `automail/A/mechanism-detail.png`, whereas the source's rear whole view and exploded frame show an exposed mechanical core. The issue is the major structure and its exposure, not a shortage of small bolts or texture detail.
2. **The opened service cover does not reveal a service compartment.** `automail/A/service-open.png` exposes a solid faceted surface where the source's service-port detail shows internal machinery. A detachable-looking cover by itself does not satisfy the source's service-access construction.

The socket intrusion in `automail/A/socket-depth.png` and the coarse hand construction also merit revision, but the two gaps above independently explain the failure without imposing finished-art expectations.

**B meets the declared visual prototype criterion.** It contains the complete shoulder, exposed actuator/core, elbow, tapered layered forearm, accessible service compartment, wrist and segmented five-digit hand, with the source's defining ivory/graphite/gold arrangement. The exposed core and opened compartment are visibly constructed rather than indicated solely by surface markings. The hand is rough, but it has the necessary major parts and interprets both dorsal and palm sides.

I do not identify a demonstrated missing major source component in B. Its armor curvature, mechanical density, finger profiles, palm details and joint transitions still differ visibly from the source. The central palm inset has an awkward dark boundary in `automail/B/palm-depth.png`, and `automail/B/hand-front.png` shows abrupt angular steps at the outer dorsal plate. Those observations call for refinement; they do not establish a missing palm, missing digit or absent major joint. Hidden connections, socket fit and motion clearances remain unverified.

### Separate finished-art judgment

**Neither is accepted as finished art.** Both have visibly faceted or overly simple shells, coarse joint transitions, basic finger segments and noisy surfaces in the supplied renders. B's open mechanics are a major structural improvement, but its shell edges and palm details still need cleanup and a closer finish to the source. A requires the missing construction to be addressed before surface refinement can complete it. B's prototype acceptance does not assert production topology, UVs, skinning, dynamic hydraulics or physical performance.
