# Independent visual review: jacket candidates A, B, C

## Scope and basis

I viewed the complete `reference.png` first, then all six supplied Cycles renders for each anonymous label: front, back, front three-quarter, rear three-quarter, left, and right. This review uses only those images. The candidates share render conditions, but their images are not registered or calibrated to the design sheet. Apparent proportions can be compared qualitatively; source dimensions, topology, manifoldness, editability, rigging, and engine readiness cannot be verified here. Dark backgrounds are display of transparency and are not treated as geometry defects.

The reference calls for a dark, fitted cropped jacket with a genuinely folded collar and lapels, continuous set-in shoulders, shaped sleeves, layered buckle cuffs, and a clearly legible shield-and-chain arrangement on the wearer's left. Front, back, and labelled-left reference views govern the decoration side where detail labels conflict.

**Verdict: none is accepted as a faithful finished jacket from these views. B is the most usable visual prototype for continued refinement; C is also a usable but less resolved prototype. A requires substantial shoulder reconstruction before it is a usable whole-garment prototype.** This is a visual verdict, not certification of any underlying asset.

## Findings shared by the candidates

- All three visibly establish an open-front, long-sleeved, short-bodied jacket with light edge trim, waist hardware, cuff buckles, and the chain motif on the correct wearer-left side. Side and three-quarter views give the body visible depth. Dark elliptical cuff interiors provide convincing open-cuff cues, although the images alone do not establish mesh construction.
- All three have comparatively planar front panels and a nearly level hem. The reference has more shaped chest and waist volume, more supple transitions around the opening, and a stronger fitted cropped silhouette. B's cuffs end closest to its hem, which weakens its cropped-body/long-sleeve relationship most among the three. This is a silhouette observation, not a measurement claim.
- None reproduces the reference's broad, turned-down rear collar and coherent collar-to-lapel fold. A and B look like upright neck bands with separately planar lapels. C introduces a more flared or rolled neck edge but still does not reproduce the source collar construction.
- The rendered main fabric reads much lighter and cooler grey than the source's near-black fabric. Silver/light trim is readily visible, but there is insufficient contrast between fabric and hardware in some lit areas. Lighting and material response both affect this result; the PNGs cannot establish the base-color values or prove which setting causes it.
- The shoulder straps, hanging decoration, buckle structure, and contrasting pale inner cuff lips remain simplified. At this render size, exact chain-link quality and tiny hardware cannot be reliably audited.

## Candidate A

### Supported strengths

- The front presents a recognizable tapered jacket outline, long sleeves, wide open center, angular lapels, and paired waist buckles (`A/view-front.png`). The sleeve drop below the hem gives a relatively convincing cropped-jacket relationship.
- Both chest and upper-arm shields are visible, with two draped chains on the wearer's left (`A/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `A/view-left.png`). Decoration is on the correct side.
- Layered cuff bands, light boundaries, and buckle frames are readable. Side and oblique views show dark open cuff interiors (`A/view-left.png`, `A/view-right.png`, `A/view-front-three-quarter.png`).
- Rear shaping/seam lines and the belt provide useful construction landmarks (`A/view-back.png`).

### Major visible defects

1. **Shoulders and armholes appear disconnected.** Black openings interrupt both shoulder joins in front and back; a particularly large open crescent surrounds the upper sleeve in the right and rear three-quarter views. Thin shoulder elements bridge visibly separated forms. This is the clearest whole-garment failure in the comparison (`A/view-front.png`, `A/view-back.png`, `A/view-right.png`, `A/view-rear-three-quarter.png`). The observation is visible separation; the exact topology causing it is unknown.
2. **The collar and lapels resemble an assembly of hard sheets.** The collar is a short upright cylindrical band, while triangular collar pieces and long lapels have abrupt corners and weakly expressed folds. Source-like folded fabric is absent (`A/view-front.png`, `A/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `A/view-right.png`).
3. **Sleeves are overly straight and smooth.** They read as near-uniform tapered tubes with a sharp cap opening, rather than the reference's continuous, shaped sleeves with restrained creasing (`A/view-front.png`, `A/view-right.png`).
4. **Ornament is skeletal relative to the reference.** The arm shield is small and simplified, chains are present, but the source's richer hanging tails and shoulder attachments are not clearly reproduced (`A/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `A/view-left.png`, `A/view-back.png`).

### Verdict

**Unaccepted whole-garment prototype.** A provides useful visual blocking and hardware placement, but shoulder continuity must be rebuilt before refinement of surface detail is worthwhile.

## Candidate B

### Supported strengths

- The shoulders read as connected garment surfaces in all six views. B avoids A's large open shoulder crescents and has the most convincing overall torso-to-sleeve continuity (`B/view-front.png`, `B/view-back.png`, `B/view-rear-three-quarter.png`).
- Sleeve irregularity and gentle longitudinal waviness supply more cloth-like form than A or C's comparatively smooth tubes (`B/view-back.png`, `B/view-right.png`, `B/view-rear-three-quarter.png`).
- The wearer-left upper-arm shield has the clearest broad face and recognizable decorative motif. Two chain swags and fine hanging elements are visible (`B/view-front.png`, `B/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `B/view-left.png`).
- Front opening, body depth, rear belt, paired hardware, layered cuff treatment, and open cuff interiors are readily readable (`B/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `B/view-back.png`, `B/view-left.png`).

### Major visible defects

1. **The armholes are pinched and lumpy.** Dark triangular dents sit near the underarm/upper sleeve transitions. The sleeve caps and top shoulders flatten or kink, rather than following the source's clean set-in seam (`B/view-front.png`, `B/view-back.png`, `B/view-rear-three-quarter.png`). These are visible shape/shading defects; the images do not establish whether they come from surface overlap, normals, or deformation.
2. **Collar folding remains unresolved.** The upright band has a flat upper silhouette, and the notched lapels read as thin planar zigzags. The source's rolled rear collar and thicker folded lapel transition are missing (`B/view-front.png`, `B/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `B/view-right.png`).
3. **The sleeve/body relationship is less close to the source.** Cuffs extend relatively little below the hem, and the upper sleeves are broad. The resulting jacket reads more compact and boxy than the reference's fitted cropped torso and longer tapered sleeves (`B/view-front.png`, `B/view-back.png`). Camera registration is unavailable, so this should guide visual adjustment rather than an exact dimensional change.
4. **Trim and attachment construction is incomplete.** The shoulder detail reads as thin strips or a small rectangular element; the reference's substantial edged epaulettes and rear shoulder hanging assembly are not clearly resolved. Waist and cuff buckles are simplified thin frames (`B/view-left.png`, `B/view-back.png`, `B/view-front-three-quarter.png`).

### Verdict

**Usable development prototype; unaccepted as a finished match.** B is the strongest starting point for the whole garment because its major parts read as connected, its sleeves suggest cloth, and its left ornament is the most legible. Armhole shaping, collar construction, proportions, and material appearance still need substantial work.

## Candidate C

### Supported strengths

- The front has a clear tapered body, longer sleeve drop than B, broad lapels, and an open center (`C/view-front.png`). It retains a plausible cropped jacket silhouette.
- The neck edge has more flare/roll than A or B's straight upright bands, and the sleeve caps are rounder (`C/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `C/view-left.png`). This is an incremental visual improvement, not a faithful source collar.
- Chain swags, chest shield, waist belt, and cuff buckles are visible on the expected sides. Cuffs show open dark interiors (`C/view-front.png`, `C/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `C/view-left.png`).

### Major visible defects

1. **Rear armhole continuity is unresolved.** A light sliver and adjacent dark wedge interrupt the viewer-right shoulder/underarm in the back view and remain apparent in the right/rear oblique views. The sleeve seems to meet the body as overlapping or disconnected layers rather than a clean sewn transition (`C/view-back.png`, `C/view-right.png`, `C/view-rear-three-quarter.png`). Overlap or penetration is a plausible interpretation, not a proven mesh diagnosis.
2. **The upper-arm shield is poorly legible.** It appears as a narrow fragment in the front and front three-quarter views, with only a partial face visible from the left, instead of the source's broad decorated shield. Hanging tails are not clearly established (`C/view-front.png`, `C/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `C/view-left.png`). Whether orientation, occlusion, or actual shape causes this cannot be settled from the images.
3. **Cuffs read as plain cylindrical extensions.** Bright upper and lower boundaries surround broad smooth bands, but the reference's separate strap and layered cuff construction are much less apparent than in A and B (`C/view-front.png`, `C/view-back.png`, `C/view-left.png`).
4. **Collar/lapel and torso surfaces remain rigid.** The shallow flared neck strip does not create the source's broad folded rear collar. Large front panels are planar, and the side/rear body is boxy with little chest-to-waist contour (`C/view-front-three-quarter.png`, `C/view-left.png`, `C/view-rear-three-quarter.png`). Sleeves are smooth and tube-like.

### Verdict

**Usable development prototype; unaccepted as a finished match.** Its front outline is useful, but the rear armhole defects, unreadable sleeve ornament, simple cuffs, and shallow collar require reconstruction or significant refinement.

## Relative preference and next decisions

For a whole-garment development starting point, **B is preferable to C, and both are preferable to A** on the visible evidence. The strongest ordering is A's clear disadvantage in shoulder continuity. B's advantage over C rests on more readable ornament, layered cuffs, and more continuous sleeve joins; C has a somewhat more favorable sleeve drop and a more flared neck edge. This ordering does not establish superior topology or production readiness.

The next revision should first achieve a continuous shoulder/armhole silhouette and a true folded collar/lapel structure. Then reconcile the fitted cropped proportions and shaped sleeves, make the wearer-left shield and hanging elements readable in both front-oblique and labelled-left views, restore layered strap cuffs, and tune the dark fabric/metal separation under the same lighting. Those are visible acceptance targets supported by the supplied reference.
