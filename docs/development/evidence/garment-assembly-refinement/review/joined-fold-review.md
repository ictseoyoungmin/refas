# Anonymous jacket comparison

## Verdict

**Relative preference: B.** The preference is clear for the collar/lapel outline and moderate for the jacket as a whole. B makes the neck and front revers read as one understandable garment construction: the light trim follows the upper collar edge into a recognizable notch and then down the lapel. A has several separated, doubled-looking diagonal trim fragments and small overlapping pointed shapes in that same area. Those fragments remain conspicuous in the front, oblique and side close-ups, rather than resolving into the clean collar construction in the source.

**Finished visual acceptance: A — reject. B — reject.** B improves the most conspicuous local construction problem, but the shared silhouette, ornament, material and cloth-reading limitations remain substantial. This is a relative preference, not acceptance of B as a finished match to the complete reference sheet.

Confidence is moderate to high for that ordering at the supplied resolution. Confidence is lower about the exact cause of small bright slits, dark separations and hard shading transitions. They can be judged as visible artifacts without identifying their underlying construction.

## Whole silhouette and source-specific identity

Both candidates communicate the same broad garment: a black cropped uniform jacket with long outward-falling sleeves, an open front, pale piping, a waist belt, cuff buckles, shoulder tabs, and asymmetric chest/sleeve insignia with two draped chains. These features are sufficient to recognize the intended family of jacket. The body, sleeve lengths, waist taper, belts and large ornament placements look essentially the same between A and B. I do not see a whole-body proportion advantage that would independently decide the comparison.

Compared with the full source, both bodies read as a smoother, more schematic shell. The shoulder caps are softly rounded; the sleeves fall in long, nearly uniform tapers; the front-to-side transition has less of the source's shaped chest projection and fitted waist articulation. The reference has more subtle sleeve bends, local cloth compression, seam-related shaping and tailored front volume. The candidates preserve the cropped-body/long-sleeve relationship, but lose some of the source's fitted, constructed character. These are visual proportional impressions, not measurements: the reference cameras are unregistered, and the reference itself contains differently posed and illustrated views.

The larger source-specific identity loss is the ceremonial decoration. The source's chest and sleeve shields use an ornate fleur-de-lis-like emblem, with conspicuous hanging pendants/tassels, and the decorated shoulder carries hardware and a long vertical hanging element visible from the rear. Both candidates reduce the shield mark to a sparse line emblem and mostly retain only the two curved chest-to-arm chains. The hanging pendant cluster and prominent rear shoulder ornament are absent or not visibly represented in the inspected views. This makes both candidates feel appreciably plainer than the particular jacket in the sheet, even where their base silhouette is recognizable.

## Collar and lapels: why B is preferred

### A

A's upper lapel region is visually fragmented. In the front close-up, short white diagonal strokes sit above and beside the longer lapel border, with visible interruptions between them. In the oblique close-up they form a narrow doubled V-like feature on the upper face rather than a clean edge around a believable collar point. The side close-up shows several stacked or stepped pale edges. These details compete with the intended collar/lapel boundary and make it difficult to read which surface is the collar, which is the turned lapel, and how the two meet.

The problem matters at whole-jacket scale as well: A's collar area looks busy and broken while the rest of the jacket is very smooth. The source has angular collar points and layered cloth, but its piping follows those edges with an orderly, intentional path. A's extra small diagonals do not convincingly reproduce that layering.

### B

B has a much clearer single outer silhouette through the upper collar point, notch and long lapel. The front and three-quarter views read immediately as a notched jacket front. In the side close-up, the transition from the front lapel toward the neck is less fragmented. The collar also presents a discernible outward turn around the nape rather than only a small upright band with detached-looking front details. These improvements bring B closer to the source's readable collar-and-revers organization.

B is still too simplified to accept. The upper lapel faces are broad and quite planar, with an abrupt tonal boundary near their top. The neck-to-lapel transition does not yet show the source's convincing cloth roll and compact layered edge. In the oblique and side views, the rear collar's light lower edge projects above a visible dark interval and reads somewhat like a stiff horizontal shelf. That interval could represent an intended folded layer, but the supplied images do not make its physical connection entirely persuasive. The rear trim also has a shallow irregular central dip in the back view. Those issues are smaller and less confusing than A's multiple broken-looking trim paths, but they remain visible.

## Shared construction, surface depth and material limitations

1. **Ornament complexity and attachment.** The sparse shield emblems and lack of the source's hanging pendant cluster are substantial fidelity losses. The chain loops are readable, but their far attachment near the sleeve is less clearly anchored than the source's decorated assembly. Shoulder tabs are visibly present, yet their small bright tab/button details are much simpler than the source's broad dark, edged tabs and decorated shoulder hardware.

2. **Strap and buckle reading.** The waist and cuff straps appear as broad, smooth silver-gray bands. In the reference, those areas read primarily as dark straps with pale edging and separate silver buckles, holes and fittings. The candidates' bright bands blur the distinction between cloth/leather strap and metal hardware. Their buckle detail is also sparse. This is a visual material-separation issue, not a claim about exact material values under different lighting.

3. **Cuffs.** Both cuffs read as regular cylindrical bands with a strong rounded bright strip. The source has more of a dark strap wrapped around a distinct jacket cuff, with a larger readable buckle and a conspicuous pale inner/under-cuff strip. Only a narrow pale rim is apparent in the candidates, and their cuff construction looks more mechanical and uniform.

4. **Cloth response.** Both have limited local folds, compression and small depth changes. The long front pieces, sleeves and waist finish therefore look rigid relative to the source. Dark seam lines on the back and sleeve help establish panels, but do not provide enough surrounding form to carry the source's tailored cloth impression. The black base does read as cloth-like matte material in places; the issue is principally the smooth, uniform shaping and the contrasting hard-looking straps.

5. **Connections and small visible artifacts.** A narrow bright crescent/slit remains near the upper sleeve behind the lapel in both oblique close-ups, and the side close-ups show pinched-looking marks in that region. The shoulder/lapel transition consequently looks unresolved. A few tiny bright specks appear on the shoulder/upper sleeve. The images do not establish whether these are exposed layers, shading, trim or render artifacts, so I do not identify them as proven holes or intersections. They are secondary to the collar and missing identity features.

For finished acceptance against this complete sheet, the next visible result would need both convincing collar/lapel depth and the missing source-specific decoration, along with more faithful dark strap/silver hardware separation and a less uniformly rigid cloth silhouette. Fixing only the collar outline is insufficient.

## Tradeoff and uncertainty

B exchanges A's small overlapping angular forms for larger, calmer collar/lapel planes. That is advantageous for readability, but it also exposes how flat and stiff those planes still are. A retains a more upright, straight-edged nape band and more local pointed layering, whereas B has a broader outward collar lip. If an upright nape and strongly separated pointed layers were the overriding visual requirement, that tradeoff could narrow or reverse a local collar preference. It does not reverse my overall choice against this complete source: A's fragmentary piping and ambiguous fold construction are too prominent, and the source calls for both clear edges and plausible layered cloth.

The concepts and renders do not share calibrated cameras or lighting. I therefore assign no numerical likeness score, do not claim exact dimensional errors from the views, and do not treat render grain as garment texture or mesh damage. The review is limited to visible appearance. It makes no inference about editability, topology, articulation, UVs, rigging, engine readiness, authorship, chronology or general modeling/workflow superiority.
