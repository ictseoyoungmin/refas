“Finished visual acceptance” here means acceptance as a finished visual realization of the supplied reference. A relative advantage does not establish that the preferred candidate is finished.

## Evidence and limits

I inspected each complete source image before inspecting the candidate renders. I then examined the matched whole and detail sheets and the individual images listed below. I used only the supplied images in the two asset directories. The judgments concern visible form and appearance.

The matched candidate views permit direct A/B comparisons. The reference-sheet cameras are unregistered, so the reference comparisons are qualitative. I assign no calibrated likeness score or exact dimensional error. The 512-pixel renders and their visible grain limit judgments about very small seams, attachment points, surface roughness, and tiny hardware. I do not treat unseen features as absent, or infer topology, articulation, UV quality, rigging, engine readiness, or workflow/model superiority.

## Garment

### Source identity and whole silhouette

Both candidates clearly establish the broad subject: a cropped black uniform jacket with a front opening, pale lapel edging, long sleeves, shoulder tabs, buckle details at the waist and cuffs, and a one-sided arrangement of chains and shield emblems. Their main body and sleeve silhouettes are very similar, so the relative decision largely turns on the neck and lapel treatment.

Neither fully captures the reference's tailored character. The reference has more developed chest-to-waist shaping and sleeves with a more natural bend and fabric variation. Both candidates read as a smoother, more generic torso with long, relatively straight tubular sleeves. The broad front, side, and back views do not show a substantial A/B improvement in these body proportions or in the garment's overall tailoring.

The reference's identity also depends on a clearly folded, notched collar/lapel assembly, dark leather-like straps with bright metal buckles, and elaborate fleur-de-lys ornament and hanging accessories. These remain significant shortcomings in both candidates.

### Relative preference: tie

**B has the better neck-to-body continuity.** Its higher rounded collar stand and closer relationship between the collar and upper front reduce the impression of separate rectangular tabs hanging in front of the torso. This is most apparent in the fold-oblique and fold-side views. The front-opening edge is also more continuously legible in B's whole front view.

**A has the clearer edge definition.** A's white trim generally outlines recognizable boundaries of the upper collar tabs and lower lapels. Its upper pieces are overly flat and rectangular, but their silhouettes and trim remain easy to read. In B, the upper white lines break into inward slashes and overlapping V-like marks that do not consistently read as trim following the free edge of folded fabric. The oblique detail makes this especially conspicuous: a conspicuous white V sits within a broad dark surface, while other lengths of edging terminate separately.

A has its own major connection problem. Its upper collar tabs project forward like flat strips, with an obvious dark gap before the lower lapel in the side and oblique details. The relationship is less convincing than the compact turned collar and notch in the source. B reduces that separation, but the resulting upper lapel is broad and ambiguous, and the trim no longer explains the fold convincingly.

I therefore find **no clear net winner in source fidelity**. The whole silhouette is essentially tied. B improves the impression of collar continuity, while A retains a more intelligible edged-collar graphic. Both lose an important part of the reference at the same focal area.

**Uncertainty and possible reversal:** confidence in the tie is moderate. If continuity of the collar surface is the dominant criterion, B is the reasonable preference. If a clean, immediately readable pale-edged notched collar is the dominant criterion, A is the reasonable preference. Higher-resolution evidence could clarify small attachments, but the broad collar and trim tradeoff is already visible in the supplied views.

### Finished visual acceptance: A — reject

A is recognizable as the intended type of jacket, but the collar is not convincingly finished. The projecting rectangular upper tabs, abrupt separation from the lower lapels, and shallow, planar fold treatment remain prominent in both the whole and close views.

The shared body and accessory shortcomings also matter:

- The torso and sleeves have limited tailored shaping and fabric behavior compared with the source.
- The waist and cuff bands read as broad pale gray or metallic strips, whereas the reference emphasizes dark straps with bright edging and discrete metal buckles.
- The emblems are very simplified line drawings. They do not reproduce the large, ornate fleur-de-lys shields in the reference.
- The visible chain arrangement is simplified, and the reference's distinctive lower hanging tassels and pendants are not reproduced in the supplied views.
- Shoulder tabs, fasteners, seams, and strap construction are sparse relative to the reference's finished garment detailing.

These are visible design and finish deviations, not merely differences in render sharpness.

### Finished visual acceptance: B — reject

B's more continuous neck area does not resolve the collar design. The fragmented white lines and unclear relationship between the upper collar edge and lapel fold are conspicuous at normal whole-view scale and become more distracting in the details.

B also retains the shared torso, sleeve, strap, emblem, chain, and shoulder-detail limitations above. The collar treatment would need a coherent edge path and a clearly readable folded notch before it could support a finished source match. The rest of the garment would still need refinement after that correction.

## Automail

### Source identity and whole silhouette

Both candidates are recognizable as a white, dark gray, and gold mechanical arm with a large circular shoulder interface, exposed upper-arm components, a round elbow, tapered forearm armor, a wrist joint, and a five-finger hand. Their broad shoulder-to-elbow-to-wrist-to-hand hierarchy is credible, and the overall relative lengths do not produce the most conspicuous discrepancy.

The larger mismatch is the simplification of the silhouette and assembly. The reference has shaped, overlapping armor, substantial dark recesses, varied exposed mechanisms, an anatomically contoured hand, and a rich relationship between shells and inner structure. Both candidates read as a straighter, more regular stack of shells, rods, and cylindrical joint units. Their side and back views reveal a much simpler internal structure and panel organization.

### Relative preference: B

**B is the stronger visual candidate, with moderately high confidence.** Its advantages are visible in both the whole views and the matched close views:

- **Shoulder shell:** B has a continuous rounded surface that reads more like a formed cover. A shows broad polygonal bands and abrupt changes of plane across the same large white shell.
- **Forearm armor:** B's main white cover has more developed curvature and a fuller taper. It has a clearer volume relative to the darker interior. A reads more as a set of flat, angular slabs, with obvious planar shading and blunt surface transitions.
- **Shoulder socket:** B's concentric lips and stepped rim produce a stronger impression of layered depth. The surrounding segments also have more relief. A's socket is readable, but the rim and surrounding shell feel comparatively flat and mechanically simplified.
- **Palm:** B's palm housing and pads read as rounded, distinct surfaces. A's palm and pads are conspicuously faceted blocks. B is closer to the reference's softer grip-pad reading, although its pads remain too simple and somewhat swollen.

These changes improve the way the major parts occupy space. They do not substantially improve the assembly's overall complexity, finger construction, rear structure, or service-area design.

**Tradeoff that could reverse a local preference:** B softens some of the reference's crisp, engineered panel language. Its shoulder cover is broad and smooth, with fewer readable panel divisions than the source. The forearm can read as a bulbous cover plus a narrow strip, and the palm pads can look inflated. A's harder planes and sharper panel marks occasionally suggest a more angular mechanical design. If strict planar armor language were weighted above formed volume, A could be preferred in an isolated area. Across the shoulder, forearm, socket, and palm together, B's improvement in surface volume is stronger than that loss of crispness.

### Finished visual acceptance: A — reject

A remains visibly unfinished as a source match:

- Large white shells and the palm show conspicuous faceting, making the primary forms read as coarse blocks.
- The source's layered shoulder armor and complex interface are reduced to a central cover and relatively simple circular side units.
- The upper arm and elbow lack much of the reference's surrounding mechanical layering and differentiated attachment detail.
- The forearm armor is much simpler in contour, panel division, and relationship to the inner structure.
- The hand has blocklike finger segments and highly repetitive cylindrical joints. Its palm contour and pad arrangement lack the reference's detailed anatomical organization.
- The service-detail view presents a closed dark plate with a vent and bright strip. It does not demonstrate the source detail's open service bay, paired exposed mechanisms, or layered access-panel arrangement.
- The material palette is recognizable, but the broad uniform surfaces do not recreate the reference's rich separation of white armor, dark mechanisms, metallic edgework, and grip surfaces.

The whole asset is coherent enough to identify, but the coarse shell treatment and simplified component relationships prevent finished acceptance.

### Finished visual acceptance: B — reject

B removes much of A's obvious faceted appearance from the main white covers and palm. It still falls short of a finished reference match.

The shoulder shell remains overly broad and sparsely divided. Its smoother volume does not replace the source's nested panels and surrounding mechanical construction. The socket has better relief, but its visually prominent bright inner lip and simplified surrounding segments still differ from the reference's more integrated dark and metallic assembly.

The forearm remains a simplified cover arrangement, with a large smooth surface and a narrow neighboring strip instead of the source's more differentiated plates and exposed structure. The unchanged rear service plate still provides only a limited visual match to the reference's service detail. The palm pads are rounder but overly generic, and the fingers retain the same coarse, block-and-cylinder visual language.

B is a useful relative improvement in several large surfaces. It is not a finished reconstruction of the arm shown in the source.
