## Observed appearance

The new rear collar reads as an upright, continuous curved band. The shredded
nape, narrow exterior strips through the lining and long, flattened horizontal
collar projections seen in the previous fitted reference are absent from these
views. The upper edge has a controlled curve instead of a corrugated fringe.

At the front, the two notched upper lapels read as folded panels and connect
visually with the long opening toward the waist. The open-front, cropped torso
and sleeve proportions remain consistent with the source design in this
avatar-oriented T pose. The original concept uses lowered sleeves and complete
materials/ornaments, so this comparison cannot establish the final worn
silhouette or decorative identity by itself.

The diagnostic colors are unchanged: blue exterior and rust lining/rim. A folded
lapel intentionally exposing its lining is not automatically a defect. No new
large crossing, detached cloth fragment or flattened collar wing is apparent
in these particular views. Small lining contacts remain a known geometric
issue, as disclosed in the stage audit.

## What the stage reports establish

The author reports a maximum collar fit displacement of 31.309 mm and body
displacement of 32.326 mm, using 347 collar cross-section rows with 12 vertices
each. The complete section receives a shared displacement, preserving its
relative profile instead of independently snapping nearby folded leaves onto
the body envelope. This agrees with the visible improvement.

The retained audit reports zero outer self-crossing pairs, while 583 collar and
46 body outer/lining contact pairs remain. Those are read results from this
frozen stage, not newly rerun independent measurements. They do not complete
the final U2 checks or permit relaxing the planned positive-thickness repair.

## Attachment and integration checks still required

The nape appears close to the body surface with a narrow lower-edge shadow, but
that image does not establish exact seam contact. In the script,
`maxSourceAttachmentDistanceM` measures original collar row-zero points against
the original body before transfer. After the section displacement is diffused,
it is not a measurement of the **current collar-to-fitted-body** attachment gap.
The final frozen stage should record the current declared anchor locations and
clearances rather than using that source-distance field as an attachment pass.

The coherent fit also changes the displacement field used for trim and metal
placement. On integration, lapel piping, seams, chain anchors and buckles need
to follow the repaired fit. Merely substituting new body/collar meshes while
retaining decorations moved by the earlier distorted fit could leave stale
attachment locations. The wearable author was informed of this risk.

The next acceptance review should use the frozen positive-thickness repair and
the integrated garment with its actual avatar, ornaments, declared motion and
export. No full-suite rerun or additional geometry test was performed as part
of this preliminary appearance review.
