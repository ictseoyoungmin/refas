# Anonymous garment visual review

## Verdict

**Preference for further development: B. Finished visual acceptance: neither A nor B passes.**

B is the stronger development base because its near-black body and smoother visible shoulder-to-underarm transitions are closer to the reference's tailored jacket appearance. It also has a less conspicuous cylindrical neck opening than A. These are visible advantages in the supplied renders, not evidence about how either candidate was constructed.

The preference is qualified: B's small upper collar folds are less clearly readable as broad cloth panels than A's lighter triangular pieces. Both candidates retain major departures in collar/lapel construction, torso shaping, fabric behavior, and ornament identity. Neither reads as a finished realization of the whole source sheet.

## Whole-source fit and proportions

Both candidates establish the recognizable category: an open, waist-length uniform jacket with long separated sleeves, contrasting edge trim, waist buckles, cuff straps, shoulder tabs, and an asymmetric badge-and-chain arrangement. The open front and the negative spaces between torso and sleeves remain legible in the front and three-quarter renders.

However, both front views read as a relatively tall, straight-sided opening bounded by very straight lapel edges. The source has a more emphatic cropped, fitted-waist impression, broader folded upper lapels, and more shaped chest volume. In the candidate three-quarter and side views, the front is comparatively flat and slab-like, with limited breast/chest shaping and little cloth response around the waist. This is a qualitative silhouette comparison; the source projections and candidate camera setup are uncalibrated, so I am not claiming a measured length or circumference error.

Both candidates' sleeves are dominated by long, smooth tube-like surfaces. The source has subtler tailoring, elbow-area bends and folds, and a more natural shoulder cap. Candidate sleeve-to-torso negative spaces are clean but rather rigid and regular. The back panels preserve useful center and side seam cues, yet the backs feel unusually taut and smooth.

**Supporting views:** both front, front-three-quarter, left, right, back, and rear-three-quarter images; source front/side/back renders and detail strip.

## Candidate A

### Strengths

- The overall open jacket silhouette, cuff bands, paired waist buckles, contrasting piping, shoulder tabs, and asymmetric chest/upper-arm ornament locations are easy to identify.
- The relatively light rendering makes the large front lapel panels and separate upper triangular pieces easy to read. The notch between the upper and lower pieces is visible in front and front-three-quarter views.
- Back and sleeve seam lines provide some tailoring structure. The open cuffs are visible from the side and oblique views.
- The chest chains form two distinct drapes, and the upper-arm shield provides a clear ornament silhouette at the supplied image size.

### Defects

- The body appears medium gray and somewhat satin-like under the supplied lighting, substantially lighter than the source's near-black cloth. Broad shoulder and sleeve highlights reinforce a synthetic shell impression.
- The collar reads as a conspicuous straight cylindrical band around the neck. The source instead shows a broad folded collar with a shaped roll into the lapels. A's upper triangles and long lapel planes do not convincingly join into that continuous folded relationship.
- The back and rear-three-quarter views show pronounced dark, pointed indentations at the underarms. Combined with rounded shoulder bulges, these make the visible arm-to-body transition feel pinched and less natural than the source. This is an appearance judgment, not a topology finding.
- The front opening is tall and very regular, and the long straight lapel edges flatten the torso impression. Side views show limited front-body curvature.
- Cuffs and waist bands read as bright, glossy bands. In the reference they are chiefly black straps with fine light edging and discrete metal buckles.
- The source's fleur-de-lis shield, detailed linked chains, pendant/tassel cluster, and hanging shoulder strap hardware are heavily simplified. The candidate has the general shield-and-chain idea but not the full ornament identity. The rear-visible shoulder/drop ornament in the source is not clearly resolved in A's back view.
- Small fasteners, strap holes, the pronounced shoulder-tab hardware, and the light cuff lining visible in the source are absent or too weakly resolved to judge as faithfully represented.
- The broad sleeve and torso areas lack the source's cloth folds and localized fabric tension.

**Primary evidence:** front and front-three-quarter for gray body, lapels, front gap, and ornaments; left/right for neck band, flat torso profile, and tubular sleeves; back and rear-three-quarter for pinched-looking underarms, taut back, and weak rear ornament identity.

### Finished acceptance

**Fail.** A communicates the intended garment family, but the neck/collar form, visible underarm pinching, cloth appearance, and incomplete ornament detail remain material visual defects.

## Candidate B

### Strengths

- The near-black body is substantially closer to the source's main cloth appearance, and the silver/white edging and ornaments stand out more appropriately against it.
- Back and rear-three-quarter views show smoother visible shoulder and underarm transitions than A. The sharp triangular dark pits seen in A are less evident, and the rear silhouette reads more continuously.
- The collar is less dominated by an exposed cylindrical ring. Its rear profile and shoulder-edge relationship are closer to a shaped collar, although the fold remains incomplete.
- The same useful garment cues remain legible: open front, separated long sleeves, waist buckles, cuff straps, seam lines, shoulder tabs, upper-arm shield, and two draped chains.
- Small lapel fasteners are visible in the front view and contribute useful detail.

### Defects

- The upper collar and lapel junction remains the largest form problem. In front and front-three-quarter views, narrow angular strips and zigzag light outlines crowd the neck. They do not read as the source's broad, supported folded cloth panels. One near-side upper fold in the front-three-quarter view appears particularly narrow and upright.
- The body and front opening remain comparatively tall, straight, and flat in appearance. The fitted cropped silhouette and chest shaping of the source have not been convincingly recovered.
- The sleeves and back are still very smooth and tube-like/taut. The darker shading improves the cloth impression, but it does not supply the source's visible drape, elbow folds, or soft tailoring.
- The bright cuff and waist bands remain visually dominant and too metallic-looking compared with the source's black straps and fine trim. The light inner cuff/lining detail of the source is not clearly represented.
- Ornament identity remains simplified: the shield motif lacks the source detail at this resolution, pendant/tassel elements are not adequately resolved, and the source's rear-visible shoulder/drop hardware is not clearly present in the back view. Two chain drapes alone do not reproduce the full ornament assembly.
- Broad upper chest and shoulder areas remain sparse, with shoulder-tab and strap details less convincing than the source's shaped pieces and hardware.

**Primary evidence:** front and front-three-quarter for dark cloth, collar crowding, front opening, and ornament simplification; left/right for relatively flat torso and sleeve profile; back and rear-three-quarter for smoother visible underarms, taut back, collar rear profile, and missing/weak rear ornament detail.

### Finished acceptance

**Fail.** B is the preferred development base, but the collar/lapel form, tailored volume, fabric folds, and defining ornament details still require substantial visual refinement.

## Relative drawbacks and possible regressions

No temporal order is known, so these are relative tradeoffs rather than claims about chronological regression.

- **B versus A:** B loses some of the easy planar readability of A's upper triangular collar pieces. In B's front and front-three-quarter views, the upper trim becomes a crowded zigzag arrangement, and the very dark surfaces make the folded panel widths harder to read. B should not be treated as a complete collar improvement simply because the exposed neck ring is less obvious.
- **A versus B:** A's lighter body and stronger glossy highlights move the cloth appearance farther from the source. A also shows more conspicuous pinched-looking rear underarms.
- **Shared unresolved issues:** the main proportions/negative-space impression, flat torso shaping, rigid sleeve appearance, overly bright strap bands, and incomplete ornament identity remain. B's strengths do not resolve these shared shortcomings.

## Development priorities

1. Rebuild the visible relationship between the neck roll, broad collar leaves, notches, and lower lapels so that they read as folded cloth from front, side, and rear views.
2. Refine the cropped fitted torso and front opening together, restoring chest volume and a more persuasive waist transition while preserving the useful open-jacket silhouette.
3. Develop natural shoulder-cap and underarm shaping, then add restrained cloth behavior around elbows, armholes, and waist. Preserve the smoother visible transitions that favor B.
4. Restore the defining ornament assembly: legible shield motif, plausible chain attachment points, pendant/tassel cluster, shoulder-tab hardware, and the rear-visible hanging strap/drop detail.
5. Bring cuffs and waist straps into the same dark cloth/leather family as the reference, keeping the bright material emphasis on thin piping and metal hardware.

## Limitations

This review is based only on the complete reference image and the twelve supplied 512-pixel candidate renders. Lighting, material settings, exposure, render noise, and camera projection can affect perceived color, shape, and continuity. The source contains stylized views and drawings that are not calibrated to the candidate cameras. I therefore make no numerical likeness claim and no claim of measured dimensional accuracy.

Fine chain links, crest details, small fasteners, and fabric texture cannot be conclusively evaluated at this resolution. A detail that is not resolved in a render is not proof that it is absent from the asset. Apparent smoothness or pinching does not establish mesh continuity or defects.

No conclusions are made about manifoldness, editability, rigging, export readiness, physics readiness, or other unseen implementation properties. This is anonymous visual evidence and a development preference, not human approval.
