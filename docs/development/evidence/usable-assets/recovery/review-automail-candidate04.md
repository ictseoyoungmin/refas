## Solid topology defect found after consolidation

The saved native topology report contains 288 degenerate triangles in
`Delivery_AURIX_ROOT_Recess_interiors`, 48 in
`Delivery_SHOULDER_PIVOT_Recess_interiors`, and 48 in
`Delivery_ELBOW_PIVOT_Recess_interiors`. These are outside the declared lettering
exception. The current inspector's solid-topology gate rejects these counts.

The independent probe measured **all 384 areas as exactly zero**, with duplicate
world-coordinate points in every affected triangle. They are not merely small,
nonzero features below a conservative area threshold. For example, vertices 493
and 495 of the root recess compound coincide exactly in triangle 918. The full
bounded numerical observations are in
`review-automail-candidate04-degenerate.json`; the reproduction script is
`review_automail_degenerate_probe.py`.

The source-part audits before consolidation reported no degenerates. Therefore
those earlier per-part counts are insufficient to establish final-mesh quality.
The author independently identified excessively clamped bevels on short hex
socket cylinders and is preventing their collapse locally before consolidation.
No global hardware weld is recommended.

The lettering compound separately contains 1,020 boundary edges and two
inconsistent-winding edges. Open source glyph surfaces explain the boundary
classification; the two winding edges still require an explicit classification.
An exception for lettering must not become a statement that the complete asset
has zero winding observations.

## Original mechanism preservation and attachment

`review-automail-candidate04-mechanism.json`, generated by the separate
`review_automail_mechanism_probe.py`, compares the actual recovered source with
the candidate's detailed precursor and then checks delivered rigid transforms.

- All 321 original mesh names remain in the detailed precursor. None are added
  or missing. All 316 parts outside the five documented rebuilds have identical
  evaluated world vertices, triangle counts and parents at neutral pose.
- The five deliberate rebuilds are the palm chassis, two wrist annuli and two
  upper hydraulic end caps. They are reported separately from decimation.
- All 46 original parts selected by mechanism-related names remain. Bounds and
  original authoring code show upper actuators/hoses wholly on the upper segment,
  forearm mechanisms on the forearm, and tendon guides on individual finger
  segments. `Upper_rear_rod_bridge` is also segment-local. There is no deleted
  original two-anchor elbow linkage.
- The delivered candidate contains no invented `Elbow_link_*` objects. Removing
  the rejected scaffold therefore follows the source design rather than hiding
  a failed original mechanism.
- The actual elbow fixed axle and central rotor overlap about the source X axis
  at z = 0.426 m. Their modeled bounds substantiate an attached rotary assembly;
  this is not a load or manufacturing-clearance certification.
- The original wrist pivot was at z = 0.184 m, while actual gimbal geometry is
  centered at z = 0.193 m. The candidate moves the pivot by 9 mm while preserving
  all unchanged neutral geometry exactly. The rotating stem reaches the gimbal
  and retains contact through the declared motion.
- Across all 121 saved poses, maximum corresponding axis-center separation is
  1.264e-9 m at the delivered elbow and 4.215e-8 m at the delivered wrist. Detailed
  precursor axis directions also remain parallel throughout.

The current part-name mapping omits only the separately retained palm chassis;
that is a bookkeeping gap, not a missing mesh. The author is adding the missing
entry in the next candidate.

## Visual and motion scope

The examined neutral views show the source's ivory, graphite and gold grouping,
compact round elbow, continuous four dorsal hand plates and recessed palm. No
detached hand-cover fragment or obvious dorsal chassis penetration is visible
in these views. Neutral views cannot establish grip clearance or concealed
contact throughout motion.

The declared animation samples a 0–90 degree elbow reach, ±12 degree wrist motion,
45/55/30 degree finger flexion and 18/35/20 degree thumb flexion over 121 frames.
The saved inspector's zero intersections cover only 18 named armor transition
pairs and the dorsal palm chassis. They do not cover every cross-digit or
palm/proximal-thumb pair, all bearings, intra-segment details or exhaustive
self-intersection. Thus the zero count cannot close all of U2 or U4. Posed and
additional contact observations are required for the repaired final candidate.

## Retopology and optimization interpretation

The palm's connected longitudinal section rings and the periodic wrist annuli
are deliberate topology work serving actual chassis clearance and closed
joint surfaces. Capping inherited upper hose boundaries is a separate repair.
Round-hardware decimation and reduced bevel segments are optimization, not a
claim of complete limb retopology. No production UV atlas was authored; existing
source data and detailed native are retained.

The candidate contains 86,560 triangles and 99 material primitives within the
predeclared limits of 110,000 and 180. The highest reported bidirectional sampled
surface deviation is 0.423727 mm within the fixed 0.6 mm budget, measured against
the detailed functional precursor. Sampling is not an exhaustive Hausdorff
bound and does not measure the deliberate functional rebuild against an
unchanged original. Source preservation was assessed separately above.

The exported GLB maps all 86,560 oriented triangles across all 99 meshes back to
the saved native, with maximum mapped vertex error 6.007e-8 m. UV/normal vertex
splits therefore are not evidence of cracks. Exact export correspondence also
preserves actual native defects, including the zero-area recess triangles; it
does not supersede final topology validation.

U5 has supported local retopology evidence, and the arm portion of the fixed U6
budgets is supported. Full U2/U4 acceptance and the both-assets U6 gate remain
open until the repaired final candidate and jacket are inspected.
